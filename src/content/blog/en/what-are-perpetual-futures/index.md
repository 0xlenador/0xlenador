## **What are perpetual futures contracts and how do they work?**

Perpetual futures contracts are financial derivatives that allow you to speculate on the price of an asset without needing to own it, **and without an expiration date**. Unlike traditional futures contracts, they do not expire, meaning you can hold your position indefinitely... as long as you can cover the necessary margin.

These contracts are very popular in the cryptocurrency world because they offer flexibility, high liquidity, and the ability to trade with leverage. But how they function has peculiarities that are vital to understand:

- **They have no expiration date**, so there is no need to renew or roll them over.
- They stay in line with the spot market price thanks to a mechanism called the **funding rate** (we will talk more about this later).
- They can be used to open **long or short positions**, allowing you to profit in both bullish and bearish markets.

This type of contract was popularized by platforms like BitMEX and later adopted by giants like Binance, Kraken, and many DeFi platforms.

## **Key differences between traditional and perpetual futures**

Although they may seem similar, there are **fundamental differences** between a traditional future and a perpetual one:

| Feature                        | Traditional Future           | Perpetual Future              |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Expiration date                | Yes                          | No                            |
| Needs renewal/rollover         | Yes                          | No                            |
| Based on a reference index     | Yes                          | Yes                           |
| Price adjustment mechanism     | Adjustment by expiration     | **Funding rate**              |
| Liquidity                      | Variable (based on expiry)   | High (continuously tradable)  |

For example, if you buy a traditional futures contract on BTC that expires in June, when that date arrives, the contract ends. Conversely, a perpetual contract on BTC simply **stays open** as long as you meet the margin conditions.

This not only gives greater freedom but also allows you to build more sophisticated strategies, like the one we'll see later: the **delta neutral** strategy.

## **What is the funding rate and why does it matter?**

The **funding rate** is one of the most important and often **least understood** elements of perpetual futures.

It is a periodic payment between traders holding long and short positions. Its goal is **to keep the price of the perpetual contract close to the spot market price**.

- If the contract trades **above** the spot price, long traders pay the shorts.
- If it trades **below**, short traders pay the longs.

This mechanism means that, in essence, holding a perpetual position is not "free." You are constantly earning or paying depending on the funding rate.

From my experience, this is a **double-edged sword**. If you trade with leverage, the funding rate can eat into your profits if you're not paying attention. But you can also **use it strategically to your advantage** if you know how... or as in my case, if you use it **for purposes beyond leverage**, like qualifying for airdrops.

## **Advantages and risks of trading with perpetual contracts**

Like any powerful financial instrument, perpetual futures offer **interesting opportunities**, but also involve **real risks**.

### Advantages:

- **Total flexibility**: you can hold your position as long as you want.
- **Leverage**: multiply your exposure without needing full capital.
- **Allows profiting in bear markets** by opening short positions.
- **High liquidity**, especially in markets like BTC or ETH.

### Risks:

- **Automatic liquidation** if your margin doesn't cover your losses.
- **Funding rate costs**.
- **Extreme volatility**, especially in cryptocurrencies.
- **Emotional management**: trading with leverage can be stressful.

That's why, although many traders use them to seek quick profits, my approach has been completely different.

## **Delta neutral strategy: how to avoid risk smartly**

To execute this idea safely, I apply a **delta-neutral strategy**. What does this mean?

It consists of opening two opposing trades on the same asset, so that **your price exposure is zero**. Example:

- Open a **long position on Hyperliquid** (expecting it to go up).
- Open a **short position on Paradex** at the same time (expecting it to go down).

By doing this, the net effect of the price movement **cancels out**, and the only thing that matters is **meeting the volume or active time conditions** on the platform granting the airdrop.

This strategy has allowed me to:

- Minimize risk
- Increase my exposure to potential airdrops
- Not rely on technical analysis or market timing

Mind you, it's important to monitor that both platforms have reasonable fees and similar funding mechanisms.
